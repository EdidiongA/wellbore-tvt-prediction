## An Empirical Information Ceiling for Blind-Zone Stratigraphic Tracking in Horizontal Wells

**Introduction.** Automated continuation of interpreted stratigraphic position (TVT) beyond the correlated interval of a horizontal well is a prerequisite for geosteering automation and for basin-scale use of legacy laterals, where the uninterpreted "blind" zone is typically three quarters of the wellbore. We report a method, a validation protocol, and a characterization result from a campaign of twenty-seven scored evaluations on a 773-well corpus with a hidden ~200-well test population: the method reduces blind-zone MAE from a 15.88-ft constant-continuation baseline to 8.91 ft; the protocol explains, quantitatively, why several locally-validated mechanisms failed in deployment; and the characterization establishes that the residual error is limited by the information content of the inputs rather than by the model class.

**Method.** The change of variables u = TVT + Z separates smooth geological structure from the exactly-known well path: over 773 wells, a 2,000-ft moving average of u costs ~3 ft while high-pass-filtered TVT and Z correlate at −0.74, so one smooth curve is estimated and TVT = u − Z restores all high-frequency content (Figure 1). A second-order (u, dip) hidden-state model over 30-ft blocks — dip persisting at zero cost, dip changes penalized (κ = 300) — encodes locally-constant structural dip; first-order smoothness fails on this data because it taxes genuine drift. Emissions compare GR to a merged reference: the typewell, affine-mapped on the known zone, blended at weight n/(n+3) with a pseudo-typewell binned from the well's own known-zone samples — the mechanism responsible for the largest single transfer gain of the campaign. Five semi-independent decode branches (drift-cancelling, typewell-only, combined, spatial, pre-branch ensemble) are fused per station by inverse variance of their disagreement, so a GR-independent structural field — inverse-distance gradient fits over neighboring wells' known zones, predicting blind dip at r = 0.98 in held-out testing — receives weight up to 0.95 precisely where the branches scatter. The same field used *discretely*, as a DP prior, won every local arena (random 200-well holdout 6.58 vs 6.94; 2,500-ft pad-holdout 7.84 vs 8.14; corruption cohorts) and lost 0.8 ft on hidden wells — the campaign's founding design law: proportional field usage, never discrete. Runtime ~4 s/well, single CPU core, no training.

**Validation protocol.** A deliberate constant-continuation probe (15.88 hidden vs 11.84 local) calibrated a transfer law, hidden ≈ 0.845 × local + gap, which forecast later submissions to ~0.1 ft (v14 predicted 9.6, scored 9.624) and converted each score into a mechanism diagnosis via the residual gap (Figure 3). A properly-powered synthetic-corruption suite (20 wells × 4 shapes, 80 paired baselines) was promoted to veto authority after flagging, uniquely among all gates, a refinement that regressed 0.1 ft hidden; it subsequently justified the best-scoring version against a clean-validation wash (suite −0.35 MAE, paired-t p < 0.001, 55/80 wins; hidden 8.913).

**The ceiling.** Three lines of evidence. First, convergence: a bidirectional-GRU sequence model on identical inputs — with the target reformulated as the increment u − u_lastknown, worth 10 ft of OOF by itself — reaches 6.9–7.1 ft out-of-fold against the tracker's leave-one-well-out 5.51 ft (spatial structures rebuilt excluding each evaluated well; exclusion audited by nearest-neighbor distances, median 468 ft). Second, seven pre-registered exploitability probes: model routing (oracle 0.86 ft, cross-fitted gain 0.000), blending (+0.08, under floor), configuration selection (oracle 0.56, selection negative), visible-prefix self-calibration (structurally invalid at 10:1 pseudo/real zone asymmetry; selection −0.25), per-station residual correction (drift diagnostics correlate with error to Spearman 0.60 yet the signed residual is unlearnable; gain 0.0005), per-well slope correction (oracle 0.80 ft; slope unpredictable, Spearman 0.095), and typewell-derived slope estimation (the sole CI-positive local effect, +0.082 [0.023, 0.139], ~10% of oracle). Third, a twenty-submission deployment-side dose-response campaign: shipping the CI-validated typewell correction degraded the hidden score by 0.389 ft (best-ever local, worst-ever hidden), while two regularization axes — position-ramped damping toward the near-blind trend and high-frequency shrinkage toward a running mean — each exhibited interior hidden-side optima (S\* ≈ 0.13; h\* ≈ 0.46), and a third — a signed-drift ramp found by leaderboard triangulation, four probes on one parabola (δ\* ≈ −1.5) against an opposing local prior — and its profile located by exponent search at approximately a cubic — carrying the score from 8.913 to a final **8.720 ft**. On both axes the hidden optimum sits at roughly twice the local optimum with several times the depth; per-well interpreter-smoothing coefficients α (blind-zone median 0.09, known-zone ≈ 0, within-well transfer ρ = −0.068) supply the mechanism and close the per-well adaptive direction; and a diversified four-arm final slate — dose extrapolation, doubled smoothing width, an extended in-model retune, two adaptive schemes — returned 8.739–8.751, closing six directions at the plateau; a ground-truth known-zone damping target failed decisively (10.30), and the near-blind prediction line stands as the correct contraction target (Figure 5).

**Conclusions.** Blind-zone TVT prediction from single-well logs plus one typewell reaches an empirical information ceiling near 5.5 ft in-distribution and 8.72 ft under the benchmark's population shift (the last −0.18 contributed entirely by deployment-side measurement that local validation opposed or could not see); the demonstrable 0.8–0.9 ft of per-well heterogeneity beyond it is unpredictable from every internal signal and only ~10% recoverable from the typewell. Two architectures with disjoint inductive biases converge to this regime, and direction-controlled dose-response locates and closes the hidden-side optimum surface, which is displaced from — and invisible to — local validation. Further accuracy requires additional measurement — azimuthal GR, offset-well logs, resistivity — rather than additional modeling; and wherever deployment populations drift from interpreted archives, we commend transfer-law probing and paired directional perturbation as standard validation practice.
